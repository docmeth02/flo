# Ownwave

Ownwave is a Navidrome client for Apple Watch and iPhone. The watch app is standalone: it signs in to your server directly and needs no phone nearby, streams over Wi-Fi or cellular, and plays the albums and playlists you downloaded to it when there is no connection at all. The iPhone app plays the same library with its own downloads, shows in its Taste tab what smart shuffle has learned about you, and comes with CarPlay, Home Screen widgets and a Control Center control that starts a mix. Both apps keep a queue going on their own with smart shuffle built from your listening history. Siri, Shortcuts and the watch's Action Button can start a mix, play an artist, or like, boost and avoid whatever is playing, in English and German.

Each app works without the other. With both installed, the iPhone can sign the watch in to the same account, share its quality, Keep Playing and mix settings with it, and send albums to it for offline listening.

Ownwave requires Navidrome 0.64 or newer, for its new ids, the scrobble history and the OpenSubsonic transcoding and playback report extensions. The iPhone app runs on iOS 26 or later, the watch app on watchOS 10 or later.

Ownwave started as a fork of [flo](https://github.com/kepelet/flo), the open source Navidrome client for iPhone by Kelompok Penerbang Walet, and keeps its MIT license; LICENSE keeps the original copyright.

Building needs Xcode 27 with the iOS 27 SDK. Open `Ownwave.xcodeproj` and run the "Ownwave" scheme for the iPhone app, which embeds the watch app and the widgets and is the one to archive for TestFlight, or the "Ownwave Watch App" scheme on a watch simulator or device. The code both apps share lives in `Shared/`. CarPlay and the App Group the widgets read from need their entitlements on your own developer team.

## Smart shuffle

Your listening history lives on the server, not on the device. Navidrome keeps one row per counted play for every client you use, and each app mirrors that log and keeps it current, so a fresh install knows your taste from the first launch and plays from the web or another client count just as much as plays from the wrist. Only skips stay on the device that saw them, since the server has no notion of them. What you hear counts as a play after half the song or four minutes of listening, whichever comes first, the same rule Navidrome applies itself; seeking ahead does not count.

Play Something builds a mix of fifteen songs that is mostly what you play a lot right now, with recent months weighing more than old ones, and about one song in six from the less played side of the artists and genres you already live in. Keep Playing, when it is on in Settings, takes over when a queue ends: it continues from the last song you heard to the end, favours that song's artist and the genres of the session, keeps to those genres as long as enough songs share them, and explores about a third of the time, a little more as a long session goes on. Once there is any history to go on, neither mode draws at random from the whole library. A song you have heard is held back and returns gradually, with about two thirds of its weight back after three days; a different version of it is held back for a few hours. Mix variety in the iPhone's Taste tab changes that: Familiar rests a song for about a day and explores less, Adventurous for about a week, with a third of Play Something exploring. The watch follows it while it shares the iPhone's settings.

Ratings are the explicit lever. Hold the heart on the watch's Now Playing screen, or use the stars on the iPhone's, to boost a song, keep it out of smart shuffle, or clear the rating; the same two actions are offered as like and dislike in the system Now Playing controls and through Siri. On Navidrome's five star scale, five and four push a song forward, three changes nothing, two makes it a rare pick and one keeps it out of smart shuffle altogether while leaving it playable from its album; avoiding the song that plays also skips it. A song without a rating of its own takes its album's, else its artist's. Ratings set in the Navidrome web interface arrive with the next sync, and ratings set in the app are sent to the server, also when they were made offline. The heart marks a favourite, and liked songs, albums and artists count in a mix's favour too.

Why this song, on by default in the iPhone's Settings, shows the reason for each song of a mix in Now Playing and Up Next, and in the watch's Up Next. Settings in both apps also has a Diagnostics screen that shows the server history as the app sees it, the latest requests with their timing, and the reasons behind the last mixes, song by song. Its share button sends the whole log as text, which is the quickest way to describe a problem.
